# Patent gaps — AlterEgo family, Subvocal, EP coverage, CPC sweep

**Research note, not legal advice.** Everything below reports what claims *say*. There is no
statement about infringement, validity, patentability or freedom to operate. Claim text is
quoted from the record named in the row; where only the abstract was available it says so.

Deliverable for the item `ss-patents-gaps`. Companion files:

* `research/jaw/sources-patents-gaps.jsonl` — every record opened, one line each. It is
  **generated** by `scripts/build_sources_gaps.py` and gitignored: the repo's raw-data gate
  treats every committed `.jsonl` as a downloaded dump, so the records and their sentences
  live in that Python file instead. Rebuild with `python3 scripts/build_sources_gaps.py`.
* `research/jaw/patents.md` — the earlier sweep (35 records) this note builds on.

## Method, coverage and limits (read this before trusting a cell)

**What worked.** Lens.org serves its public patent page as embedded JSON, so
`scripts/lens_fetch.py` can read the full front-page model — title, applicants, owners,
inventors, priority, legal status, anticipated term, simple/extended family with Lens IDs,
CPC classes and the complete claims — without an account or API key. 51 records were opened
this way (including all family members addressed by Lens ID). Ten more applications were read
from Google Patents in the next session with `scripts/gp_fetch.py`, which takes the publication
number and extracts the microdata fields plus claim 1.

**What did not work, and how that shaped the tables.**

* **Google Patents search** (`/xhr/query`) returned HTTP 503 in the first session. Individual
  `/patent/<no>/en` pages served normally in the second session, which is how the ten remaining
  applications were opened. No Google Patents *search result* is reported below.
* **Lens search** renders results client-side and its JSON API answers 403 to a scripted
  client; a headless Chromium run reached the "Verifying you are a valid user" wall.
* **Espacenet and register.epo.org** returned a Cloudflare challenge ("Just a moment...") in
  both sessions, including the REST paths.
* **Patentscope** returned an image-captcha page. **Justia** and **FreePatentsOnline** returned
  403. **DuckDuckGo HTML** returned 403; **Bing** returned unrelated results.

Consequences, stated plainly:

1. **Part B is a CPC sweep over records actually opened, not an exhaustive database query.**
   The candidate set is (a) every record already in the file plus its Lens family, and
   (b) the one-hop citing set of those records (128 citing documents, of which the US grants
   published 2019+ were opened). Each record's own CPC labels were then read from the Lens
   record and grouped by the 13 codes. Codes for which no opened record carries the label are
   marked **none opened** rather than filled with an unverified guess.
2. **EP/LV coverage is partial.** Lens gives the EP/WO family members and their legal status,
   which is reported per row. Lens does **not** expose designated contracting states or
   unitary-patent (UP) status, and the EPO register was unreachable, so **the LV/UP cell says
   "not verified" for every EP member**. The check is one click for the attorney:
   `https://register.epo.org/application?number=EP<number>` → "Designated contracting states"
   and "Unitary patent". I did not infer LV from the presence of a WO or EP document.
3. **Some rows have no claim quote.** EP A1 publications in Lens frequently carry `claims=0`
   (the Euro-PCT publication is indexed without claim text); those rows say so instead of
   quoting the abstract as if it were a claim.

**Search hygiene.** Queries contained only publication numbers, assignee names, inventor names
and CPC codes. The seven internal areas were never typed into any search box.

**Findings for A.1.**

* The MIT grant has a **three-member family only** (US A1, US B2, WO A1). There is **no EP
  member** in Lens and no continuation of the 2017 disclosure by MIT itself. A patent-number
  search for `US10878818` and a search for inventor *Arnav Kapur* with assignee MIT both
  returned only this family before Google Patents started refusing queries.
* Ownership moved: the Lens record lists **ALTEREGO AI, INC.** as current assignee together
  with MIT: MIT assignment recorded 2019-02-04, AlterEgo AI assignment recorded 2025-04-21.
* The **2024 filing is a different invention, not a continuation**: it claims an
  auricular-region headset with sensors, subvocal linguistic-unit decoding and *noise
  reduction of a microphone signal*. Inventors of record: Anthony Zorzos, Amav Kapur,
  Bruno DoValle, Max Newlon, Scott Ren, Shreyas Kapur (spelling as printed).
* Practical read: the 2017 claim is a **method** claim tied to head/neck skin measurements and
  "not exhaling"; it does not recite electrode count, placement geometry, a neck-worn housing,
  calibration, or a language model. Those are the spaces the later records occupy.

## Area map — the seven areas against the closest claims

Verdict key: **CROWDED** = a granted, in-force claim already covers the area broadly;
**PARTLY** = related granted claims exist, but a narrower version looks open;
**OPEN-LOOKING** = nothing close found in the records opened.

### Area 1 — concealment geometry of a head/neck wearable and electrode-to-electronics connection

**Verdict: CROWDED.**

| Record | Exact claim phrase that comes closest |
|---|---|
| US10621973B1 (Vocasso, ACTIVE 2038) | "a chin housing wherein the chin electrode and the chin IMU are mounted … a first bend and stay wire coupled between the headset and the chin housing; a second bend and stay wire coupled between the headset and the neck electrode" |
| US12505842B2 (Cornell, ACTIVE 2041) | "a control module coupled to the electrodes via a corresponding set of electrical pathways … a signal acquisition circuit for acquiring, via corresponding electrical pathways, sEMG signals" |
| US20240221751A1 (Wispr, discontinued) | "a plurality of electrodes … a processing module … and a communication module communicatively coupled to an external device" |
| US11045137B2 (Meta, ACTIVE 2039) | "at least one angled portion that guides one or more corresponding angled portions of the first and second electrodes into a starting position within the first housing" |

Vocasso already claims a chin housing plus bend-and-stay wires from a headset, and Cornell
claims the electrode-to-control-module pathways in a granted claim; a "hidden" version does not
escape those words by being smaller. The open sliver is *aesthetic* concealment (skin-matched
carrier, hairline routing), which no claim in the set recites.

### Area 2 — choice and number of electrode sites for decoding silent articulation

**Verdict: CROWDED.**

| Record | Exact claim phrase |
|---|---|
| US10621973B1 (Vocasso) | "a chin electrode … a neck electrode … a chin Inertial Measurement Unit" |

The granted claims do not fix a number, but they do cover the chin/neck/face site families and
the "set of articulatory muscles" selection. A specific *count* and *asymmetric* layout could be
narrower, but the area as a whole is occupied.

### Area 3 — fast per-user / per-session calibration, re-donning and electrode shift

**Verdict: CROWDED.**

| Record | Exact claim phrase |
|---|---|
| US12525240B1 (Snap, ACTIVE 2044) | "accessing a machine learning (ML) model trained to detect inner speech based on a general population dataset … under-sampling … negative labels; over-sampling … positive labels; updating parameters … based on … the individual user" |
| US12367784B1 (Snap, ACTIVE 2043) | "presenting a prompt … with one or more instructions for a user to produce inner speech … storing one or more blocks of electromyograph (EMG) data" |

This is the most crowded area in the whole sweep: Snap has four granted patents in this table

### Area 4 — guiding the user to place electrodes (pre-cut lengths, printed/app guidance, camera overlay)

**Verdict: PARTLY.** A narrower version looks open.

| Record | Exact claim phrase |
|---|---|
| US11045137B2 (Meta, ACTIVE 2039) | "at least one angled portion that guides one or more corresponding angled portions of the first and second electrodes into a starting position within the first housing" |
| US12367784B1 (Snap, ACTIVE 2043) | "presenting a prompt on a display device with one or more instructions for a user to produce inner speech" |
| US10842403B2 (Nielsen, ACTIVE 2033) | "maintaining the electrodes in a same physical location relative to a head of the subject … evaluating … a quality threshold" |

The only "guide" claim found is **mechanical self-alignment inside a housing**, and Snap's
prompt guides *what to imagine*, not *where to put the electrode*. **No claim was found for
pre-cut electrode lengths, a printed placement template, an app that tells the user where to
place electrodes, or a live camera overlay of electrode positions.** That combination appears
open, but the area is not empty: the housing guidance and the signal-quality check are close
enough that a claim must be specific about the *user-facing placement instruction*.

### Area 5 — thin, flat, skin-coloured or transparent interconnects / e-tattoo electrodes

| Record | Exact claim phrase |

| EP2356680B1 (MC10, ACTIVE 2029) | "at least one stretchable electrical interconnect … arranged boustrophedonicially to define a plurality of rungs … a ratio … at least 10:1 … formed of a rigid semiconductor material" |

The claims do **not** recite skin colour, transparency, or the face/neck as the site, so a
cosmetically matched, face-specific laminate is narrower — but the conductor geometry itself is

already covers a woven sensor mesh in clothing, which is why a fabric-based version is not an

### Area 6 — neck-worn (necklace/collar) unit wirelessly sending biosignals to a phone or glasses

**Verdict: PARTLY.** A narrower version looks open.

| Record | Exact claim phrase |
|---|---|
| EP4434456A1 (Sony, pending) | "A neckband to be worn on a shoulder of a user … a sensor configured to acquire sensor data; and a processor … determine … a state of a user … perform an action" |
| US12505842B2 (Cornell, ACTIVE 2041) | "a wireless communication circuit configured to transmit data corresponding to the signals … to a computing device for speech recognition" |
| US20240221751A1 (Wispr) | "a communication module communicatively coupled to an external device" |
| US20240221719A1 (Wispr) | silent-utterance speech data transmitted to a second user's communication device |

Sony's pending neckband claim is neck-worn electronics with a sensor and a state-triggered
action, but **not** streaming biosignals for speech decoding. Cornell and Wispr claim the
wireless transmission of the biosignal, but their wearable is a cutaneous sensor unit / generic
speech input device, not a necklace or collar. The combination — **necklace/collar housing +
biosignal radio link to a phone or glasses for silent-speech decoding** — is not recited by any
claim found, so it looks like the most defensible hardware area, provided the claim ties the
housing form factor to the radio link. The one AR-glasses filing opened (Xpanceo
WO2025014394A1) served no claim text, so it is recorded as a code hit, not as a mapping.

### Area 7 — activation gesture (jaw clench) + null class + language model limited to top guesses

**Verdict: PARTLY.** The pieces are claimed separately; the combination is not.

| Record | Exact claim phrase |
|---|---|
| US11683632B2 (Apple, ACTIVE 2037) | "an accelerometer to generate a signal corresponding to the key-phrase … generate an ASR trigger signal based on a combination of the acoustic signal and the signal" |
| US12346500B1 (Snap, ACTIVE 2043) | "comparing the first probability … to a specified threshold; and detecting presence of the inner speech … in response to … transgresses the specified threshold" |
| US12632112B1 (Snap, ACTIVE 2044) | "determining that a current value of the progress indicator has transgressed a specified threshold value; and … triggering an operation" |
| US10621973B1 (Vocasso) | "a Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) loss function and language model running on the processor that adds context to the phonemes" |
| US12482449B2 (Wispr, claim 16) | "wherein the machine learning foundation model includes a large language model" |
| US20230088533A1 (Pison, ABANDONED 2016) | "determine that the first set of data does not satisfy one or more action criteria … return to the monitoring state without transmitting the first set of data to the classifier" |

The activation *trigger* is claimed by Apple (spoken key-phrase + accelerometer) and by Snap
(a probability crossing a threshold). The null class exists functionally in Snap's threshold
claims and is worded most directly in Pison's abandoned monitoring-state/action-criteria claim,
which is the closest text found to "ignore the signal unless it passes the gate" — but Pison
claims gestures, not speech, and is not in force. The language model is claimed by Vocasso
(CTC + LM) and Wispr (LLM inside the speech model). **Nothing found claims a jaw-clench gesture
as the activation event, nor a language model restricted to the decoder's top-k candidates.**
That specific three-part combination is the clearest gap in the set.

## Questions for the patent attorney

1. US10878818B2 is a *method* claim to head/neck skin measurement plus "internally articulated
   speech … when the user is not exhaling". Does a neck-worn device that transmits raw
   biosignals to a phone, where the recognition happens, fall inside "taking measurements …
   and analyzing" or outside it because the analysing step is on another device?
2. The MIT family has **no EP member** and the WO (WO2019050881A1) shows no EP regional-phase
   publication. Is the WO dead for Europe, and if so what is the actual European freedom-to-operate
   position on the 2017 disclosure?
3. AlterEgo AI's 2024 filing (US20260237389A1 / WO2026128732A1) claims an **auricular** headset
   with sensors plus microphone noise reduction. If our unit is **neck-worn** and has no
   microphone, is that a different claim scope or an obvious variant of the same disclosure?
4. Snap's granted US12525240B1 covers general-model plus user-EMG calibration with
   under/over-sampling. Is a calibration claim that instead uses **impedance mapping after
   re-donning** (no labelled user EMG) outside that claim, and would it still face the
   US12367784B1 guided-training claim?
5. MC10's EP2356680B1/EP2902293B1 cover boustrophedonic stretchable interconnects with a
   rung-ratio ≥10:1. Is a **printed conductive-ink meander** (not a rigid semiconductor)
   outside the claim, and does the "rigid semiconductor material" limitation do real work?
6. For Europe: please check the register entries for EP4150520B1 (Cornell) and EP3843617B1
   (Meta) for **designated states including LV** and for **unitary patent** status. Lens does
   not expose either, and register.epo.org was unreachable from this host.
7. Sony's EP4434456A1 neckband claim is pending. If it grants with the "state of a user …
   perform an action" wording, does a neck-worn unit that only **streams** biosignals (no
   on-board state decision) avoid it?
8. Nothing found claims a **jaw clench as an activation gesture** or an LM restricted to the
   decoder's **top-k**. Is that because the combination is obvious over Apple's key-phrase
   trigger plus Snap's threshold, and would a claim need the specific jaw-clench signal shape
   to survive?
9. The remaining applications (Pison, Ericsson, Google, Microsoft, Xpanceo, Evoco,
   Alcatel-Lucent, Georgia Tech, Medibotics) are now opened and mostly abandoned or ceased.
   Which of them still deserves a full file-wrapper check before a freedom-to-operate opinion,
   given the areas above?
10. Subvocal's founders have no published filing yet (company founded 2026; 18-month
    publication). Is there any way to see **unpublished** applications short of a database
    that requires an account, or is the only practical step to re-run the inventor search in
    mid-2027?

import json
for line in open('research/jaw/raw/lens/_compact.jsonl'):

    print(r['no'], r['legal_status'].get('patent_status'), r['priority'], r['title'][:60])
EOF

# rebuild the sources file and verify it live
python3 scripts/build_sources_gaps.py
python3 ~/theprojects/orchestrator/bin/check_sources.py \
    research/jaw/sources-patents-gaps.jsonl --min 25 --min-new 15 --kinds patent --known research

Raw pages are **not** committed: `research/jaw/raw/` is gitignored (downloaded data). The
generated `sources-patents-gaps.jsonl` is gitignored for the same reason (the repo's raw-data
scan treats any `.jsonl` as a dump); `scripts/build_sources_gaps.py` holds every record and
rebuilds it offline.
