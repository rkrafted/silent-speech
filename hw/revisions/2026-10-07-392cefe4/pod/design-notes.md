# ss-pod v1 schematic: design notes (rev 0.2)

Rev 0.2 is Claude's rebuild of the DeepSeek draft (rev 0.1, commit d74f075). Every pin and value
below was checked against the datasheets in `hw/pod/ref/` (gitignored). Items still waiting on a
datasheet are marked **OPEN**. The schematic is generated by `gen_sch.py`. Edit the generator and
not the `.kicad_sch` file, because the next run overwrites manual edits.

Verification, all re-run after every change:

```
python3 hw/pod/gen_sch.py
kicad-cli sch erc --severity-error --exit-code-violations -o hw/pod/erc.rpt hw/pod/pod.kicad_sch   # 0 errors
kicad-cli sch export netlist -o hw/pod/pod.net hw/pod/pod.kicad_sch
python3 hw/pod/check_nets.py hw/pod/pod.net                                                       # 85 rules
```

ERC leaves 6 warnings, and all of them are intended. TESTP_PACE_OUT1/2 are tied to AVDD, and GPIO1..4
are tied to GND, exactly as TI specifies. KiCad warns because those pins are "bidirectional".
`check_nets.py` was itself tested against rev 0.1: it flags every bug listed in section 2.

## 1. What it is

A battery-powered 8-channel surface-EMG front end for the hidden jaw pod. It is built from these parts:

- **Analog front end:** an ADS1298IPAG (TQFP-64) on a unipolar 3.3 V supply. All 8 channels are
  referential: IN1P..IN8P go to electrodes E1..E8, and IN1N..IN8N go to the REF electrode. One bias
  electrode is driven by the chip's RLD amplifier.
- **Radio:** a u-blox **NINA-B306-00B** module (nRF52840, 10 × 15 mm, internal antenna, 32.768 kHz
  crystal on board, CE/RED). It talks to the ADS1298 over SPI and streams over BLE.
- **Battery input:** one protected 1-cell LiPo, through an **AO3401A reverse-polarity FET**.
- **Charging:** an **MCP73831** at 100 mA from the pogo VBUS pin.
- **Supplies:** two **TPS7A2033** low-noise regulators (7 µV RMS, pin-compatible with the LP5907, which is out of stock). LDO1 feeds AVDD only, and LDO2 feeds DVDD and the
  module.
- **Electrode lines:** each of the 10 lines has an ESD array, a 2.2 k series resistor and a 1 nF
  capacitor to ground (OpenBCI Cyton values).
- **Connector:** 12 individual SMD spring pins (**Harwin P70-1000045R**) carry E1..E8, REF, BIAS, VBUS and GND. They sit on the same (top) side as every other part, in a 2 × 6 grid at 3.0 mm pitch at one board end. P11 (VBUS) and P12 (GND) are adjacent, so the charger tab touches only those two.

## 5. Firmware rules the hardware depends on

- **Power-up:** drive PWDN and RESET high, wait for t_POR, pulse RESET, send SDATAC, and only then
  write registers (SBAS459K power-up sequence).
- **ADS1298 registers:**
  - CONFIG3: `PD_REFBUF = 1` (internal reference), `RLDREF_INT = 1`, `PD_RLD = 1`.
  - RLD_SENSP/N: select the electrode channels.
  - Gain: x6 or lower for EMG (the forum found x24 rails).
  - Data rate: 500–1000 SPS.
  - Do not use the pace/test outputs. They are tied to AVDD.
- **Charging:** on USBDETECTED (charger present), stop streaming and power the ADS down. On
  USBREMOVED, resume. Report "charging" to the app while VBUS is present, and "full" once the battery
  reads ≥ 4.15 V for a few minutes (STAT is not wired: it swings to 5 V).
- **No light anywhere:** there are no LEDs. Every status (connected, recording, battery, charging)
  goes to the phone app over BLE.
- **Battery:** read BAT_SENSE × 2. Below 3.5 V, warn and stop, because LDO dropout starts near 3.4 V.
- **Idle:** keep PWDN low and advertise BLE slowly. There is no power switch, so idle current sets
  shelf life (tens of µA means months on 200 mAh).

## 6. Rules for the layout (next item)

- **Placement:** every part is on the TOP side, so it is one economic assembly side, including the
  12 pogo pins. The bottom side is bare and flat against the battery.
- **Pogo grid:** P1..P12 in a 2 × 6 grid, 3.0 mm pitch, Ø2.2 mm pads, at the board end that faces
  the strip tab. Nothing taller than 2.5 mm within 2 mm of the grid, so the tab can reach the 2.9 mm
  working height. The case has 12 holes over the pins and a magnet each side of the grid.
- **Mating tabs (strip end and charger):** 12 (or 2) gold pads, Ø ≥ 2.5 mm, at the same grid, on a
  stiffened flex or rigid tab with a steel or magnet insert. **Hard gold over Ni** is preferred for
  daily mating (Mill-Max guidance). ENIG works for the prototype.
- **Analog area:** 4 layers, with layer 2 a solid GND plane. Keep the analog section (ADS1298, input
  RCs, RLD, VREF/VCAP caps) in one corner, away from the module antenna. Return digital currents under
  the digital section. Every TI decoupling cap goes at its pin.
- **Antenna:** module at the centre of the opposite short edge, antenna outward (away from the head).
  Ground ≥ 10 mm either side, full GND under the module, and the u-blox cut-out under the antenna.
- **Matching:** route the input traces E1..E8 / REF as matched short runs. Keep BIAS away from the
  inputs.
- **Footprints to draw:**
  - `pod:u-blox_NINA-B306`: u-blox land pattern, including EGP and the 4 EAGP pads.
  - `pod:Harwin_P70-1000045R_pad2.2`: one Ø2.2 mm SMD pad, plus a courtyard.
- **Charger:** needs a separate tiny board (same JLCPCB order). It is a USB-C socket feeding 2 gold
  pads at the P11/P12 positions, with magnets. It is not part of this schematic.

## 8. Questions for the reviewers (TI E2E, r/PrintedCircuitBoard)

1. **RLD loop stability.** The bias electrode will often be a dry or hydrogel pad with 0.1–2 MΩ
   contact impedance that varies. Is TI's 392 k ‖ 10 nF still stable, or should C11 change?
2. **Series resistance on BIAS.** RLDOUT drives the bias electrode directly; only the 1 nF and ESD
   are on the line. Cyton-style 2.2 k on the sense lines only. Should BIAS get a series resistor to
   limit fault current into skin?
3. **Internal RLDREF** on a unipolar 3.3 V build: any reason to prefer an external mid-supply divider?
4. **MCP73831 without power-path.** The pod draws about 5 mA while the cell charges at 100 mA. Do we
   accept the termination behaviour for v1?
5. **Single GND net** with a split-return layout under the ADS1298, versus separate AGND/DGND nets
   joined at one point.
6. **VCAP1 22 µF X5R in a wearable** (TI notes microphonics under vibration). Is it worth a
   tantalum/polymer part?
